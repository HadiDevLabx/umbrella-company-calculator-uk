# Umbrella Company Calculator UK — Where the Day Rate Actually Goes

> The rate quoted to an umbrella is not your gross pay. Employer National Insurance at 15%, the apprenticeship levy and the umbrella's margin all come out before your own tax.

Reference notes for the UK tax year **2026/27**, computed from HMRC's
published rates rather than copied from another site.

## How it works

The deduction chain, in order:

1. **Assignment rate** — what the agency pays the umbrella
2. Minus the umbrella's **margin**, typically £15 to £30 a week
3. Minus **employer National Insurance** at 15% above £5,000
4. Minus the **apprenticeship levy** at 0.5%
5. Minus employer pension contributions
6. = your gross pay, which is then taxed normally

A £500 day rate does not mean £500 of gross pay. After employment costs it is closer to £430.

This is why an umbrella rate and a permanent salary are not comparable numbers, and why "outside IR35" rates look so much better.

## What this does not cover

Holiday pay is inside the assignment rate, either rolled up or accrued. If it is rolled up you are being paid your own holiday money back, not given extra.

## Source and tax year

Figures are for the 2026/27 tax year, which runs 6 April 2026
to 5 April 2027, and were checked against
[gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/rates-and-thresholds-for-employers-2026-to-2027) on 28 August 2026.

Rates change every April, and often at a Budget. Check the tax year a figure
belongs to before relying on it.
